# Roadmap

What is planned or under consideration for later versions of the lab, grouped by area. Each item says what it is, why it matters and where it came up. Items that change existing results need a minor or major version ([README](../README.md), Versions); the others can come in a patch. The order within a group is no priority.

When an item is done, it moves out of this file into the [journal](journal.md), with the date and what it changed.

## Strategy

**State (2026-11-28).** The default engine is pooled discovery (`--engine pooled`; [model.md](model.md), Strategy engine). Since 2026-11-22 its moves can be checked directly: a false positive is a move that buys nothing, or costs, against what the others actually cast ([model.md](model.md), Checking strategy: false positives; `tools/falsepos.py`). The check showed that a large part of the strategy the engines produce harms the voters who use it, so the strategic results of some methods and conditions say less about the method than about the engine. The problems found and the ways out under consideration are listed first; the older items follow. The measurements are in the [journal](journal.md), 2026-11-22 to 2026-11-28.

### Known problems

- **Moves taken by the luck of the polls.** A voter takes the most promising of about a dozen moves, each valued on the round's polls (10 since 2026-11-23, 5 before). A move that gains only in these polls is taken as readily as one that gains in general. At the defaults the share of movers whose move is a false positive falls with the number of polls (5 / 40 / 160: Plurality 38 / 32 / 25%, IRV 79 / 65 / 56%, STAR 40 / 29 / 21%, Score 9 / 7 / 5%) and half as many voters move. Raising the second choice (compromise, levelling it up) and push-over are what fails; levelling the others down and burial mostly pay. More polls reduce the problem at a cost in run time and do not remove it.
- **With every voter strategic nothing holds these moves back.** Always strategic voters have no cost of voting insincerely, so every move with any promised gain is taken. At `--strategic 1` 63–69% of the movers under Minimax and under an add-on whose count walks from leader to leader (Leader-AV) are false positives, and in the true count they lose three to eight times as often as they win. Results at strategic share 1 are therefore to be read together with the false positives, and not as the method's worst case.
- **The assumed share of followers is wrong where it matters most.** A move is valued as if `--coordination` (0.25) of a voter's type made it. With adaptive voters about that share follows; with every voter strategic the whole type does ([strategy-design.md](strategy-design.md), Why a New Design). Valued consistently (`--strategic 1 --coordination 1`) the movers lose much less (mean gain under Leader-AV-5 −0.8% of their utility range against −3.4%), but most moves still fail (63% against 69%) and nearly twice as many voters move.
- **Counts that depend on a path.** Where the winner depends on an order of steps (IRV's eliminations, a walk from leader to leader), small changes of a poll change the winner, so lucky promises are frequent. IRV's false positives stay at 56% with 160 polls, and choosing and valuing a move on separate polls does not lower them (82%). Stale polls, the poll view and the coalition's size are ruled out; the cause is open.
- **The arithmetic and exact engines promise gains for moves that fail.** They are kept for comparison (and campaigns need the arithmetic one). Since 2026-11-23 no move buries the voter's own favourite, a partial fix: under the arithmetic engine the few movers under Schulze and Minimax still lose (93–95% false positives), and under the exact engine MJ keeps ballots with the three leaders tied at the top. The fault is in their estimate of the polls (a projection and a table of how races interact), not in single moves.
- **What the polls stand for is undecided.** If the round's polls are the voters' whole information, overreacting to a lucky reading is part of what a method has to withstand, and the false positives belong in the report. If they are a sample of a belief whose spread is the poll error, the sampling error is an artefact of the simulation and should be removed. The fixes below depend on this choice.
- **Dark horses beyond the top three.** Types are defined by the poll's top three. A check found the engine elects dark horses anyway and that moves for any set of rivals among the three find no more (journal, 2026-11-22); dark horses among more than four strong candidates, and types that know more of the poll, are not covered.
- **A figure to measure again.** "About 4% of the voters move per poll under Plurality" ([model.md](model.md)) was measured with 5 polls per round.

### Proposed solutions

- **Decide what the polls stand for** (above), before any change to how moves are valued.
- **Value moves by tie probabilities** (Myerson and Weber 1993; Laslier 2006). A ballot's value is the sum over pairs of candidates of the chance of a tie between them, times what the ballot adds to one over the other, times the voter's utility difference. For additive methods (Plurality, Approval, Score, Borda) the chances follow from the poll and its spread without sampling polls, so no move is taken by luck, and lowering one's favourite can never gain. Two steps: (1) a reference condition with no free parameter for Plurality, Approval and Score: every voter plays the best reply under Laslier's order of ties (the leader rule), iterated over the rounds and judged by the false-positive check, which also shows where that order fails in an electorate of 99; (2) the arithmetic engine rebuilt on the smooth version (tie chances from the poll's spread, a coalition guess for the size), which would also fix its faults above. Limits: it gives the direction of a move, not whether it is worth leaving the honest ballot, and it has no direct form for counts whose decisive events are not ties between two totals (IRV, Condorcet methods, runoffs). Step 2 changes results of the arithmetic engine.
- **A share of followers that matches what happens.** Instead of a fixed `--coordination`, value a move with the share of the type that made it in the round before (a quarter to start with). Removes the mismatch at strategic share 1 without a new parameter. Changes results.
- **False positives in the results.** Now a diagnostic (`tools/falsepos.py`, about 60% more run time with 500 polls per coalition). As a column of the results and the report, at least for the reference runs, every strategic result could be read with it.
- **How much the false positives cost.** A trial run in which voters drop the moves that fail the check, to see how far VSE\* and WME\* move: a move that harms those who make it can still raise or lower everyone's welfare, so the false-positive share alone does not say how far the strategic results are off.
- **Trace IRV.** Follow single elections in which a compromise backfires, comparing what the engine promised with what the cast ballots give, to find the cause still open.
- **Remove the inertia engine** (`--engine inertia`, [strategy-design.md](strategy-design.md)). It had the most false positives of all engines (30% under Score, 49–78% under most methods), its movers gain about nothing on average, and it is not developed further. Its design note stays as a record; what it set out to solve (the fixed share, strategy without coordination) is in the items here.
- **Tried and left out.** A second look (choosing a move on one set of polls and valuing it on another) overcorrects: it suppresses moves whose gain shows in few polls, above all Plurality's compromise (journal, 2026-11-06 and 2026-11-23). A test of a move's gain against the poll's uncertainty counts that uncertainty twice, since the chances already contain it (removed 2026-11-04). A threshold on the gain for always strategic voters would be an honesty cost under another name.

### Other items

- **Review the strategy model with Approval in mind.** Under the default conditions Approval hardly moves from its honest result (base: 86.3 / 90.4 honest, 86.3 / 90.3 strategic). Strategic approval voting is usually expected to move it towards the Condorcet winner, even with only part of the voters strategic. Either the model misses moves that real approval voters make (approving a second candidate against a front-runner they dislike, or withholding approval from a rival of their favourite), or the expectation does not hold in these electorates. *Findings 2.1.0, section 11.* Changes results.
- **Strategy without coordination: bounded rationality.** With coordination 0 nobody votes strategically, since a single voter's move never pays its cost (findings 2.1.0, section 5.2). Real voters vote tactically anyway, also when their vote cannot be expected to matter: some naive burial on ranked ballots, compromise out of habit. As in the beauty contest game, people with limited time for thinking act partly irrationally, and a model of strategy needs some of that. Possible forms: a share of voters who make the obvious move for their type whatever it is worth, or a lower cost for moves that are easy to think of. Needs care, since any such rule decides how much each method is hurt. Changes results.
- **Campaigns with pooled discovery.** Campaigns now need the arithmetic response (`--engine arithmetic`), which misjudges pairwise counts (findings 2.1.0, sections 5.8 and 10.2). With pooled discovery, campaigns could be tested against the default engine. Once they are, the arithmetic response could be kept for comparison only. Changes results of campaign runs.
- **Smarter campaigns.** Campaigns that plan with the method's count, or across rounds, would have more effect than the current ones; the campaigns test showed little effect except under Plurality. Changes results of campaign runs.
- **Learning across elections.** Strategy now starts from a sincere poll, or from a previous election's result in the first-poll test. Repeated elections with the same electorate, where voters remember what worked, are not modelled (findings 2.1.0, section 10.5).

## Candidates and Nomination

- **Costly clones.** A clone costs its party nothing in the model, while a real one needs a second credible candidate and splits a campaign; the no-nomination test may overstate what clones do (findings 2.1.0, section 10.1). A cost for fielding a clone, like the honesty cost for voters, would make nomination more realistic. Changes results.
- **Nomination in the race: open points** (built 2026-11-18, `--race-cost`; [model.md](model.md), Strategic nomination). Nomination before the race still foresees sincere ballots exactly and runs to an equilibrium; it could become the race's first step, judged by the first polls with the same cost. The cost is one number for exits and clones alike, while leaving a race and fielding a second candidate cost differently in reality. A withdrawn candidate cannot come back. Campaigns and the dummy election of `--start previous` keep a fixed field.
- **Candidate fields closer to real ones.** In real data a few serious candidates are graded low by about half of the voters and the others by most of them: a cliff, while the model's candidates spread evenly (journal, approval data). Most voters probably have several candidates at the bottom of their scale, which is where rated methods are hit by nomination (journal, 2026-11-06). Better fields need data; candidate dispersion and shift are the current stand-ins.

## Methods and Compositions

- **More intermediate steps as parts**, so that methods can be compared on what they share, independently of their tie-breakers. Since 2026-11-08 the lab has the Schwartz, CDTT, Banks and uncovered sets, IMDS's steps and MJ's median ([compositions.md](compositions.md)). Still missing, for example: MJ's tie-break and MAV's grade-by-grade count as parts; DMS's depths.
- **A brute-force reference method.** Take the winners of all methods as a new field and hold a new election among them under all methods; repeat as long as the set changes. When it no longer changes, drop the candidates with the fewest wins (or break the tie otherwise, or by random ballot). Where the methods agree, this finds what they agree on; as a reference it shows how far a single method is from the consensus of all.
- **Multi-winner results.** Compositions that end in a set of k winners (committees) with the same machinery ([compositions.md](compositions.md), Next).

## Ballots

- **A realistic finer score range.** The score range changes the results across much of the field: on 0–5 the score–pairwise hybrids lose part of the voters' pairwise information to ties, on 0–99 several of them match the Condorcet methods (findings 2.1.0, section 6.2). 0–99 is impractical on a real ballot, 0–9 or 0–10 is not. The reference runs include 0–9 again since 2026-11-08 ([reference-runs.md](reference-runs.md), max-score); the next findings should state the score range's role wherever score methods are compared. Possibly a reason to reconsider the default range. Changing the default changes results.

## Measures and Analysis

- **Strategic information for every method.** The working notes on strategic information (repository contextuality-voting-paradox, `notes/strategic-information`) compare methods by what their ballots and counts can hold, so far by hand and with scripts for single methods and three candidates. Computed in the lab, the same quantities would cover every method it defines, compositions and add-ons included. Done (2026-11-28, `tools/information.py`): what needs no strategy, the ballot polytope and the count. Open: (1) the strategic count at a poll, the count of every type's best reply, with its rank, what it holds of the leader's row and of the whole majority relation, and whether one method's is a function of another's; it needs a best reply that is defined for every method, which is the open question of the Strategy section (a ballot's value as the gradient of the expected outcome at a belief about the count is one candidate for both). (2) Polls that confirm themselves: the poll moved towards the best replies from several starts, and per method the share of profiles where the outcome depends on the start. (3) For the tool itself: counts in whole steps (MJ) and boundaries that are not flat, which it cannot measure now; the strategic vertices of two-round ballots (the round-1 marks only). None of this changes results.
- **Simplicity.** The front does not measure how simple a method is for voters, officials and the public (findings 2.1.0, section 10.4). A measure, even a rough one (steps of the count, whether it needs the ballots more than once, whether it can be counted by hand at polling stations and summed), would let simplicity enter the comparison instead of being weighed separately.
- **`--show` and `--hide` in general.** Since 2026-11-08 `--hide` applies within `--show`. Method sets that contain references are a general trap: the plots could leave out the references by default whatever selects the methods, and the report could say which methods each plot shows.
- **Identical results across machines.** The regression check can differ between machines where rounding decides a near-tie (another BLAS kernel; [tools/regression.py](../regression.py)). Rounding the values compared in a voter's choice before the comparison would make results identical everywhere, at the cost of changing a few existing ones.

## Reference Runs and Validation

- **Test Soft Minimax-D outside this model.** Soft Minimax-D was proposed in this lab and chosen among four clip rules with these simulations. Its rule has a reason that does not depend on the model (findings 2.1.0, section 10.3), but other simulators and real ballot data would be the stronger test.
